# License

Fileship is open source. Since version 6.6.0, the Fileship server is licensed under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 (AGPL-3.0) with one additional term: the "Powered by Fileship" notice on the login page stays. Fileship for Windows, the browser extension and the shared packages are licensed under MIT.

The full details are in [LICENSING.md](../../LICENSING.md). This page answers the common questions. It is not legal advice; the license texts decide.

## Can I self-host Fileship for free?

Yes. For yourself, your family, your community or your company, with as many users as you like. Nothing changes for self-hosting compared with earlier versions, except that the login page shows a small "Powered by Fileship" link.

## Can I change Fileship?

Yes. If you let other people use your changed version over a network, you must offer them its complete source code under the AGPL-3.0, for example with a link to your repository. Changes you only use yourself do not need to be published.

## Can I use Fileship commercially?

Yes, including running it inside your company and offering it as a paid service to others. A service must keep the "Powered by Fileship" notice, publish its changes to Fileship as described above, and use a name of its own: it may say that it is based on Fileship, but it may not call itself Fileship. See the [trademark policy](../../TRADEMARKS.md).

## Can I remove the "Powered by Fileship" notice?

Not under the AGPL-3.0: the notice is the one additional term of the license. You can restyle and translate it. If you need to remove it, or want to use Fileship in a closed-source product, ask about a commercial license at contact@hanzy.dev.

## What about Fileship 6.5 and earlier?

They were released under the MIT License and stay under it. The AGPL-3.0 applies to Fileship 6.6.0 and later.

## Why did Fileship change its license?

The AGPL-3.0 keeps Fileship open source while making sure that anyone who builds a service on it shares their improvements and credits the project, instead of turning it into a closed product.

## Why do I have to sign a CLA to contribute?

The [Contributor License Agreement](../../CLA.md) lets the project offer the commercial license next to the AGPL-3.0, and change its license in the future if needed. You keep the copyright of your contribution, and every contribution that ships in a release stays available under that release's open-source license.
